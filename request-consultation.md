# Request Consultation

TechNerd uses a discovery-first consultation process. The goal is to understand the business problem, intended outcome, systems or platform involved, urgency, and likely engagement fit before any work is scoped.

For governance, identity, audit readiness, remediation, advisory, website, and e-commerce work, the right engagement shape depends on context.

## What To Include

When requesting a consultation, include the details that are comfortable and relevant:

- **Name and organization:** Your name, organization, role, and preferred contact method.
- **Organization or project type:** Enterprise, mid-market, small business, nonprofit, professional services firm, healthcare practice, community organization, author, creator, personal brand, e-commerce business, or other.
- **Primary need:** Governance, identity and access, audit readiness, risk review, policy or procedure support, cloud ownership, vendor review, roadmap planning, small-organization security guidance, website design, Shopify or e-commerce, website maintenance, or custom web functionality.
- **Business driver:** Audit preparation, finding remediation, leadership concern, stale access, unclear ownership, compliance pressure, vendor requirement, security roadmap, new business launch, outdated website, e-commerce launch, rebrand, audience growth, or general improvement.
- **Systems or platform involved:** Examples include Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Active Directory, Entra ID, IAM tools, SaaS platforms, cloud resources, business applications, Shopify, an existing website platform, domain or hosting provider, or an evidence repository.
- **Timeline or urgency:** Desired start date, audit date, remediation deadline, launch date, board or leadership milestone, campaign date, event date, or whether the request is exploratory.
- **Preferred engagement type:** Assessment, advisory session, workshop, documentation support, website package, e-commerce package, maintenance plan, a la carte service, or custom consulting.
- **Known constraints:** Budget sensitivity, payment timing, availability, stakeholder access, documentation gaps, content readiness, system access limits, vendor dependencies, or nonprofit considerations.

For website and e-commerce requests, it also helps to include the current site if one exists, estimated number of pages or products, available brand assets, whether written content and images are ready, any required integrations, and the main action you want visitors or customers to take.

## Good Fit Examples

- An audit is approaching and evidence expectations are unclear.
- Access reviews are happening, but ownership, review criteria, or remediation tracking are weak.
- Application or cloud resource ownership is fragmented.
- A small organization needs practical security priorities without enterprise overhead.
- Leadership needs a clear briefing, roadmap, or risk-based sequence of next steps.
- Policies or control narratives exist, but they do not clearly connect to operating evidence.
- A business, author, creator, band, nonprofit, or professional needs a polished representative website.
- A small business needs a Shopify store configured, organized, and launched.
- An existing website needs clearer structure, modernization, maintenance ownership, or a better fit for the organization.
- A web project requires custom integrations, forms, workflows, or functionality beyond a standard package.

## Not The Right Fit

TechNerd is not currently positioned as an emergency incident response provider, 24/7 managed IT help desk, hardware repair service, consumer tech support provider, formal audit or certification authority, bargain bulk website factory, or unlimited on-demand development service.

If a request falls outside current service areas, TechNerd may decline it or suggest a better type of provider.

## How Discovery Works

1. **Initial request:** You send a brief description of the need, context, timeline, and systems or platform involved.
2. **Fit review:** TechNerd reviews whether the request matches current service areas and availability.
3. **Discovery conversation:** If there appears to be a fit, the first conversation clarifies goals, audience or stakeholders, scope, risks, content or documentation readiness, and constraints.
4. **Scope recommendation:** TechNerd recommends a package, a la carte service, advisory path, maintenance plan, or custom proposal.
5. **Clear agreement before work:** Work begins only after scope, deliverables, responsibilities, third-party costs, pricing approach, and timing are clear.
